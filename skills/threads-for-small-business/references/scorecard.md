# The four-pillar scorecard

Four pillars, 25 points each, 100 total. Presence, engagement and consistency are arithmetic: compute them, don't judge them. Reputation is the only judgement call.

Work out these numbers first from what the owner gave you:

- **posts_per_week**: posts in the period ÷ weeks the period spans. The period is the span of the posts they gave you, so ask whether that's every post or a selection; a selection makes every arithmetic pillar wrong.
- **replies**: other people's replies only. If you can tell the owner's own replies are included in the count, take them out.
- **last_post_days_ago**: days since the newest post.
- **has_posting_gap**: any gap of 14+ days between posts in the period.
- **posting_span_days**: days between oldest and newest post given.
- **avg engagement rate**: mean of each post's (likes + 2 × replies + reposts) ÷ views × 100. If no views are available, use followers as the denominator instead and say so (the "followers basis").

## Presence (0–25)

| Followers | pts | Posts per week | pts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| over 2,000 | 10 | 3 or more | 10 |
| 300–2,000 | 7 | 2 to under 3 | 7 |
| 50–299 | 4 | 1 to under 2 | 4 |
| under 50 | 1 | under 1 | 1 |

+2 if verified. +1 if the bio says something real (20+ characters, not just an emoji or handle). −3 if the last post was over 30 days ago. Clamp 0–25.

## Engagement (0–25)

Bands by average engagement rate. Interpolate inside a band so 2.9% scores higher than 1.6%.

| Views basis | Followers basis | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3.0%+ (up to 6%) | 5.0%+ (up to 10%) | 20–25 |
| 1.5–3.0% | 2.0–5.0% | 14–19 |
| 0.5–1.5% | 0.5–2.0% | 8–13 |
| 0.1–0.5% | 0.1–0.5% | 2–7 |
| above 0, under 0.1% | same | 1 |
| no posts | | 0 |

**Small samples.** When most posts have under 100 views, one reply moves a post's rate by several points, so a high engagement score says little. Keep the score, but say plainly that it rests on a handful of interactions, and don't let it carry the diagnosis.

## Consistency (0–25)

- **Recency:** last post ≤7 days ago 8, ≤14 days 6, ≤30 days 3, older 0.
- **Frequency:** 4+/week 10, 2+ 7, 1+ 4, 0.5+ 1, less 0.
- **Regularity:** no 14-day gap: 7 if the span is over 30 days, else 5. With a gap: 2 if still posting 1+/week, else 0.

Sum, clamp 0–25.

## Reputation (0–25) — judged

Start at 12. "Mentions" means posts by others about this business (tagged or not), as far as the owner knows.

- Up: +4 to +8 for clearly positive mentions (praise, recommendations, tagging friends). +2 for more than 10 mentions. +1 for a warm tone in replies (thanks, questions, return customers). +1 if average replies per post is above 2.
- Down: −4 to −8 for complaints or service failures in mentions. −3 for clearly negative words (scam, late, never arrived, refund, rude, or the same in their language). −2 if average replies is under 0.5 and there are no mentions, **only** if you actually know there are no mentions.
- No mentions and no replies: stay at 12. Don't penalise a new business.

## Confidence

Say how much to trust the scores:

Count what's missing: followers count unknown; where followers are from unknown (Threads Insights shows it); fewer than 15 posts; no mentions to read.

- **High:** nothing missing.
- **Medium:** one missing.
- **Low:** two or more missing.

If confidence isn't high, say plainly that the scores rest on limited data.

## Picking the priority

Name one improvement priority: **frequency**, **engagement**, **consistency**, **reputation** or **content variety**. Choose the highest-leverage fix given the scores and the owner's goal. On a tie, prefer frequency > engagement > content variety > consistency > reputation: an account that barely posts can't learn what works.

## How to present it

Lead with the diagnosis as one plain sentence of at most 14 words, e.g. "Your question posts win; the silence between them loses followers." Then the four scores in a small table, then one sentence on what they mean together: which is weakest and why, tied to the diagnosis. Don't explain the rubric unless asked.
