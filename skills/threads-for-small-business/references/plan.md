# Plans: 30 days, competitors, positioning

## 30-day content calendar

30 ready-to-post captions, one per day, building week by week. Follow the caption rules in [posts.md](posts.md).

- Each day: day number, format, the full caption, and its job (reach, trust or orders) plus what it tests.
- **Default week (7 posts):** 2 teaching posts (one skill or tip the audience can use today: trust), 2 shareable posts (a strong hook made to be passed on: reach), 1 true story from the owner (a real moment with feeling: trust), 1 soft sell (leads naturally to the product: orders), 1 question or poll (starts a conversation: reach). Posting less often, keep the ratio and drop from the teaching and shareable slots first.
- Then shift the mix to the diagnosis. An account nobody finds needs more reach; one with views but no orders needs trust, then orders. Never put orders posts back to back.
- Stories must be the owner's own. Ask for two or three real moments (a first customer, a mistake fixed, a hard week) before writing them; never invent one.
- Start from formats their data shows work, then vary. Week 1 repeats proven winners; weeks 2–4 test one new thing each, and say which result would keep it.
- Never repeat a caption. Never state a fact the owner didn't give you.

If 30 is too many for the conversation, write week 1 in full and outline weeks 2–4, then offer the rest.

## 4-week action plan

Operational tasks, not posts: refer to posts by calendar day instead of repeating them.

- An intro: what the plan is built around (1–2 sentences, from the diagnosis).
- Exactly 4 weeks, 2–3 actions each. Each action: what to do (specific to this business), how they'll know it worked (a number they can see in Threads Insights), and a priority (high, medium or low).
- One week-1 action is always the **first-hour reply routine** (see [posts.md](posts.md#the-first-hour)).
- Typical actions: profile fixes (bio says what they sell and how to order; a link that works), a reply routine, turning recurring questions into posts, a weekly look at what flopped, a competitor counter-move.

## Competitor watch

For each competitor or peer account the owner names (up to 8, most direct first; skip accounts with no posts):

- **Working:** what they do that works, citing their followers, bio or sample posts (max 40 words).
- **Counter:** what *this* business does this month in answer, from its own products, customers and strengths: a specific post, reply habit or offer it already has (max 40 words). Adapt, never copy.

End with a summary: what competitors are doing right now and the one move that matters most this month.

Skip platform and celebrity accounts. They aren't comparable.

## Positioning and white space

When there's enough about the niche:

- **Overview:** the niche and where the business sits in it.
- **White space:** 3–5 content angles no competitor owns.
- **Positioning:** the one to take and why it's defensible (what they have that others can't easily copy: the founder, the process, the place, the customer).
- **Quick wins:** 3 things to do this week.
