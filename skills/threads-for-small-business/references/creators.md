# Working with Threads creators

Paid posts from small creators (hundreds to tens of thousands of followers) can reach people the business's own account can't. They work when the creator's audience already wants what the business sells, and the post sounds like the creator, not an ad.

## Should they pay creators yet?

Usually not before the basics: an account that posts at least weekly, a bio that says how to order, and replies answered. A creator sends people to the profile, and an empty or silent profile wastes the money. Say so if that's where they are.

## Picking a creator

Judge a creator on fit, not followers:

- **Content style:** do their posts already look like something this business's customers enjoy?
- **Niche alignment:** do they post about the category, or about the life the product fits into (a home baker for a kitchenware shop, a new mum for baby clothes)?
- **Engagement personality:** do people reply to them, and do they reply back? Replies per post beats likes.
- **Audience location:** is their audience where the business sells and ships? A great creator in the wrong country is wasted.
- **Bio signals:** what they say they're about, any past brand work, how they want to be contacted.
- **Affordability:** for a small business, smaller creators with real conversations usually beat big accounts.

**Red flags:** audience in the wrong country, mostly giveaways or follow-for-follow, replies that look bought (generic, emoji-only, same accounts every time), long silences, a niche that clashes with the product, past posts that would embarrass the business.

## The brief

Write a brief the owner can send as is, addressed to the creator, 80–140 words:

- What the product is, in one plain sentence.
- What to show (the product in use, an unboxing, a before/after, their honest take).
- The angle: why it fits *their* audience.
- What to include: the price, how to order or the link, any offer, and the deadline. Only facts the owner gave you.

Add:

- **Hook line:** one opening line the creator could post as written, in their audience's language.
- **Do's:** 4–6 short, concrete instructions specific to this product and customer. Bad: "use a friendly tone". Good: "ask followers which flavour sells out first".
- **Don'ts:** 4–6, aimed at this business's weak spot or positioning risk. Usually: no reading a script, no claims the business didn't make.

Disclosure rules for paid posts differ by country. Tell the owner to check what applies where they and the creator are.
