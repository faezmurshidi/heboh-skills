# Posts: teardown, captions, replies

## Post teardown

Show the owner exactly what works by comparing their own posts.

1. Rank their posts by engagement rate (see [scorecard.md](scorecard.md)). Take up to the 4 best and 4 worst. When several posts tie at the bottom (often at 0%), the worst are the ones with the most views: they wasted the most reach.
2. For each: quote the opening words verbatim (up to 15), mark it **worked** or **flopped**, and explain **why**: the mechanism, citing its views, replies or rate. "It asked a question people could answer in one word" is a mechanism; "it was engaging" is not.
3. For a flop, give the rewrite that would have worked. For a winner, say how to repeat it without copying it.
4. **Spread or talk.** Among posts with real numbers, compare reposts with replies. Posts reposted a lot but barely replied to *spread*: people pass them on, so they're reach material. Posts with many replies but few reposts *start talk*: they build trust with people already following. Name one of each when the data has them, and say which kind this business needs more of, given the diagnosis.
5. Finish with the **pattern**: the one rule that separates winners from losers, in one or two sentences. Then turn it into **one formula the owner can repeat every week**: the opening style, the topic, the length and the ending that their winners share, written as a short fill-in sentence (e.g. "Open with a customer's exact question, answer it in 2 lines with the price, end by asking which one they'd pick"). Build it only from their own winners; with fewer than 3 clear winners, say the formula is a guess to test. This is the one place a fill-in shape is fine: it's a recipe, not a caption.

Common mechanisms to look for:

- Winners: a question with an easy answer ("which one would you pick?"), a relatable complaint, a before/after, a real customer moment, a price or detail shown openly, a poll-style choice, a behind-the-scenes mess.
- Flops: a product photo with only a name and price, an announcement ("new stock!"), a caption that does three jobs at once, a post that ends without anything to reply to, a link-first post.

## Writing captions

**One job per post.** Name it before writing:

- **Reach**: new people find the business. Questions, opinions, relatable moments, trending conversations in the niche.
- **Trust**: proof. Process, customer words (anonymised), behind-the-scenes, how it's made, mistakes fixed.
- **Orders**: a clear way to buy now. What it is, the price, how to order, by when.

A post that tries to reach, convince and sell at once does none of them well. Its call to action serves only its job: a reach post ends with a question, not "DM to order". Never run orders posts back to back.

Caption rules:

- Write the full post as the business would post it, ready to paste. No templates, no `[brackets]`.
- The audience's language and register: if their followers write in Manglish, so does the caption.
- The first line carries the post. It appears before "more", so it must make someone stop.
- Short. Most winning Threads posts are 1–4 lines.
- Only facts the owner gave you: prices, colours, sizes, delivery. An orders post needs a price and a way to order; if either is missing, write the caption with the owner's own wording ("around RM50"), and list what they must confirm before posting.
- Emoji only if their audience uses them.

For each caption, give: format (text question, photo, carousel, short video, before-and-after), the caption, and its job plus what it tests (max 20 words).

## The first hour

Replies in the first hour decide how far a post travels. Build the routine from **their** reply patterns, not general advice:

1. Read the replies they get. What do people ask (price, delivery, sizes)? What do they praise? What do they complain about?
2. Write the routine:
   - **Which replies to answer first, and how fast.** Usually: every question within the hour, praise with a real sentence (not just ❤️).
   - **The follow-up question** that keeps a thread going. Turn "nice!" into "thanks! which colour would you go for?"
   - **Where to share the post** after it's up: their Instagram story, WhatsApp status, a customer group, if they have one.
3. Recurring questions are content: a question asked three times deserves its own post.

## Hooks for a creator or the owner's next post

A hook is one opening line someone could post as written. It should fit how this audience already talks (look at their replies), name something specific to the product, and invite a reply. Explain in one sentence why it fits.

Curiosity works: an unfinished detail, a surprising price, a "nobody tells you this about…". Never rage-bait or fake outrage: it brings angry replies from people who won't buy, and a small business can't afford the pile-on.
